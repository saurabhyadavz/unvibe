# Paywalls and monetisation: structure before price

## The headline finding

Once a paywall works, teams want to test the price. The price is one number on a screen full of
decisions: which plans, in which order, how the trial works, how often you test. Across $3
billion of subscription data, changing the number of plans drove 63% more conversion uplift than
any price test; localising the paywall gave 62% LTV uplift; changing the trial structure 60%.
Price changes came in far lower. A weekly plan with no trial earned $7.40 of LTV over a year; the
same plan with a trial earned $54.50. "You're not losing subscribers because of your price.
You're losing them because of your architecture." [sYRhXB_ZcLI, qJ4qYhTqAz0]

## Structure moves

- **Fewer options.** Mojo's first paywall showed subscriptions beside packs of AI credits.
  Hiding the credits (no price, copy or design change) lifted new subscription revenue 14% within
  a week: the credits were an "escape hatch" that turned one decision into a comparison and sent
  would-be subscribers to small purchases. Test taking one option off the first screen.
  [qJ4qYhTqAz0]
- **Lead with the plan you want.** Built With Science put annual first and monthly one tap
  away, and annual share went from about 60% to 75 to 80% at the same prices. Whatever is shown
  first feels like the normal choice (the default effect). [qJ4qYhTqAz0]
- **Trial beats discount.** Pregnancy+ tested a discounted intro price against a free trial with
  nothing to pay up front and expected the discount to win; the trial made 140% more revenue,
  because people who used the full product converted more and stayed longer. Once used, the full
  app feels like theirs (the endowment effect). Never blur or hide the user's own content behind
  the wall; that feels like something of theirs being taken away. Undertone's new paywall blurs
  nothing and asks one question: how many sessions a month do you need? [qJ4qYhTqAz0]
- **Make the best plan hard to ignore.** Moonly offered the trial only on the annual plan and
  conversions rose 39%, revenue per 100 installs 47%, by asymmetry (the decoy effect). Headspace
  gave monthly a 7-day trial and annual 14 days; a longer trial reads as a better deal even when
  the value is intangible (anchoring). [puldme__Ckk]
- **Weekly is the new monthly.** Weekly plans went from 43% to 55.6% of subscription revenue in
  two years while monthly collapsed to 11.7%. Weekly converts 1.7 to 7.4 times better than
  annual at every price tier, because a high-intent but uncertain user will commit to "a coffee a
  week" and not to a year; by month four the annual upgrade is easy. Offer weekly first unless
  there is a reason not to. [sYRhXB_ZcLI]
- **Higher price, higher-intent buyers.** Median high-priced apps convert downloads at 2.8%
  against 2% for mid-priced; the top quartile hits 6.1%. People willing to pay more have
  self-selected as serious. Do not race to the cheapest competitor; the bottom is where
  conversion dies. [sYRhXB_ZcLI]

## Honest paywalls convert better

The default assumption is that friction reduction means hiding the cost. Blinkist added one
explicit line saying exactly when the user would be charged and for what, front and centre:
conversion rose 23%, complaints fell 55%, churn did not change. Surprised users cancel and
review badly; informed users commit harder. Any ambiguity about when they are charged, what they
get and how to cancel costs conversions and cancellations at once. [sYRhXB_ZcLI]

Explain the trial as an itinerary. Blinkist's day-by-day breakdown of the 7-day trial lifted
sign-ups 23%. Cal AI spends three screens on it inside its onboarding: when you will be charged,
a promise of a reminder before the trial ends, and the headline "No payment due now"; by the
paywall the decision is already made. People fear the unknown more than commitment.
[puldme__Ckk, sYRhXB_ZcLI]

## Timing and placement

- 82 to 89% of subscription starts happen on install day; after day seven, trial-to-paid
  conversion drops and never recovers. The first session is the game. [sYRhXB_ZcLI]
- Show the paywall after the user has felt something. It is a trust screen as much as a pricing
  screen: does it appear after value, and does it say exactly when money moves? [sYRhXB_ZcLI]
- Earn the right to ask, then ask in context. Tinder shows Boost when you are already getting
  matches, Gold when you are curious who liked you; premium enhances the core loop instead of
  interrupting it. Paywalls at moments of confusion feel like a trick and produce one payment and
  a churn. [4yKtlOWClhA, v1f9iYZ2GhQ]
- Soft-lock where deep engagement meets intent, not at the beginner's first step. Busuu let free
  users learn one language and charged for the second: no cost to casual users, an 83%
  conversion lift among the engaged. Mobbin added "Pro" labels to locked content across the
  product and free-to-paid rose 35%; make what is missing visible through contrast. [puldme__Ckk]
- The graduated gate: free with no sign-up, then attention (an ad to unlock), then money at the
  emotional peak. Each stage normalises the next and paying feels like relief, not a wall.
  Attention, then effort, then money. [7dCZrtH15zc]
- One-touch trial start: Slopes' instant trial lifted trial starts 25%. Every extra step at the
  entry point loses people. [puldme__Ckk]

## Words and framing

- "Trial" carries obligation; Headspace's "30-day guest pass" lifted sign-ups 7% with a word.
  Reframe copy to the emotion you want. [puldme__Ckk]
- A single estimated price beat a range (Uber, double-digit lift in rides). Where trust is at
  stake, be exact. [puldme__Ckk]
- Credits, tokens and units make spending feel lighter than dollars; that is how brains treat
  abstract numbers. ReelShort exploits it with variable episode prices and awkward bundle ratios
  so no one can run a mental total. The ethical version keeps the abstraction and shows a running
  total of what has been spent; the question is whether you help users stay informed or prevent
  it. [7dCZrtH15zc]

## Test velocity

Apps that ran 50-plus paywall experiments a year earned 18.7 times the revenue of apps that ran
one, in the same categories at similar prices. Top performers average about 15 a year; most
founders run zero and treat the paywall as a checkout screen. Set up one test where the price
stays exactly the same and only the structure changes: hide one option, or swap which plan is
first. One widget redesign moved Freecash's day-seven retention by several points; one surface,
one experiment, compounding. [sYRhXB_ZcLI, qJ4qYhTqAz0]

## Paywall checklist

- Plans shown: as few as the decision needs; the escape hatch removed from the first screen.
- Order: the plan you want first; others one tap away.
- Trial: present, explained day by day, with the charge date and a reminder promise on screen.
- Copy: exactly when and what they will be charged, how to cancel, in plain words, not fine print.
- Timing: after a felt moment, in context, never at a moment of confusion.
- The user's own data never blurred or held hostage.
- Price last, and higher than instinct says, tested monthly.
